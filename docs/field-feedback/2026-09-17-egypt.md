# Harness feedback from the Egypt project — 2026-09-17

Field feedback from about three weeks of running the harness on a second project: a
TypeScript game with a headless deterministic simulation, a browser render layer, one
owner who plays the build, Sonnet builders, Opus judgment roles, a Fable lead, and a
Codex CLI seat for second-vendor review and (since 2026-09-16) some builds. Roughly 190
PRs, one written post-mortem, one outside audit of the process (Codex, read-only), and
a rules rewrite on 2026-09-16 that already moved the plugin's `rules/` to "rules only".

This file covers what that project learned that applies to any project the harness
runs, not to the game. Items are ordered by how much they cost or saved. Each has the
evidence, what generalises, and the ask. Issue numbers are the Egypt tracker's unless
prefixed `harness#`. Nothing here is a ruling; it is for the harness owner to go
through and adopt, adapt or reject.

## 1. The plugin still describes the wave-lead it retired

The 2026-09-16 commit retired the wave-lead and rewrote `rules/operating-model.md` under
new headings. The rest of the plugin was not updated, so a fresh `harness-init` today
generates a `CLAUDE.md` that contradicts the operating model it points at:

- `templates/CLAUDE.md.template` lines 74 and 86: the roster lists **wave-lead** and the
  lead "delegates wave orchestration to fresh wave-lead subagents".
- `skills/harness-init/SKILL.md` lines 60 to 63 and 102: `harness:wave-lead` is named as
  spawnable and a `wave-lead` label is created.
- `templates/agent-sessions.md.template` line 12: "lead/wave-lead's job".
- `README.md` lines 4 and 47 and `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`'s description.
- Dangling section references to headings that no longer exist in `operating-model.md`:
  `§ Owner-comms policy` (ask-owner skill, harness-init), `§ Review-and-fix` (review-pr
  skill), `§ Design-led wave shape` and `§ Model policy` (harness-init, SPECIALIZING.md),
  `§ Agent-session lifecycle` (CLAUDE.md.template, agent-sessions template).
- `harness#4` (wave-lead reporting chain) is still open and is moot.

Ask: one mechanical pass over the plugin for every reference to the retired role and
every `§` citation, and a check in `verify-gates` or a test that every `§ X` in the
plugin resolves to a heading in `rules/`.

## 2. Parallel gates on one machine put every builder into timeout diagnosis

Ten builders launched at once on the night of 2026-09-16. Every full suite ran at 80 to
100 s with test-runner worker RPC timeouts on every branch; four builders spent a
visible share of their tokens diagnosing the timeouts instead of building, and one went
0.34M against a 0.25M ceiling on exactly that. The same thing happened on 2026-09-09
with twelve parallel comment-sweep agents: a slow test timed out for two agents and for
the lead's own Stop gate, all of which passed in isolation. The scorecard's read: stagger
to four or five concurrent gates.

What generalises: the Stop gate and `/harness:validate` run the full `TEST_COMMAND` per
agent with no awareness of siblings, and a builder has no instruction for what to do
when the suite times out under load.

Asks:
- A second key in `harness.env`, something like `SCOPED_TEST_COMMAND`, that builders run
  during work (the files under edit), with the full command reserved for the gate at PR
  time. Egypt's 2026-09-11 lesson "cap builders' test runs to the files under edit" is
  the same finding.
- A line in the generic builder template: if the suite times out or fails on an
  unrelated slow test while other agents are running, retry once when load drops; do not
  diagnose infrastructure.
- Operating-model guidance on concurrency: four or five gate-running agents per machine.
  A lock or a load check in the Stop gate would make it mechanical.

## 3. Token self-reports are unreliable; the parent has the meter

`token-efficiency.md` rules 6 and 9 say every agent states its own metered spend and
the lead assembles tables from what children reported. On #574 a builder self-reported
10.4M tokens; the harness meter said 0.39M (a budget-delta misread, 25x). Forks report
inherited context, so a fork's figure overstates its build (#515). The parent session
sees the real figure in the task result.

Ask: invert the rule. The parent records the metered figure from the task result; a
child's self-report is a cross-check, never the source. The scorecard column header
Egypt settled on says it: "tokens: metered by the harness, never self-reported".

## 4. A model scorecard is what makes routing a measurement

Egypt keeps `docs/review/model-scorecard.md`: one row per PR with builder and effort,
metered tokens, launch-to-PR and PR-to-merge wall-clock, rework rounds, reviewer
findings as blocking/mechanical/routed, second-vendor findings as found/confirmed, and a
one-line note. The lead routes issues on it and re-routes between checkpoints on the
record. Two findings came straight out of it:

- The Opus-builder pilot (six mechanism PRs): end-to-end time per PR the same as Sonnet
  (57 min against 61 to 66), blocking-finding rate the same (1.3 against 1.2 per PR),
  about 1.6x the cost. Sonnet stayed the default. The builder tier was not the lever.
- The review seat was. On the night of 2026-09-16 the second-vendor read found a real
  defect on six of eight Sonnet builds and on both Codex builds.

Ask: ship a scorecard template with that column set, and make "routing cites the
scorecard" a rule. `SPECIALIZING.md` currently says model-tier differences between
builders are "a cost decision to make deliberately"; the scorecard is how that decision
gets made on record.

## 5. The second-vendor seat needs its own bindings

`operating-model.md` names `codex exec review --base main` but the plugin ships nothing
for it. Operational facts learned the hard way, all stack-agnostic:

- Codex reads `AGENTS.md`, not `CLAUDE.md`. The reviewer ran its whole pilot with zero
  project context until the owner noticed. Egypt's `AGENTS.md` is deliberately thin: it
  points at `CLAUDE.md` and the rules, inlines only the build, test and lint commands and
  the two or three load-bearing conventions, and asks the reviewer to report which rule
  files it actually read "so the lead can tell a read review from a guess".
- Run `codex exec` with stdin closed (`< /dev/null`) or it waits on stdin forever.
- Its read-only copy has no dependencies installed, so it cannot run tests, and says so.
- As a builder in a workspace-write worktree it cannot commit: the worktree's git index
  is outside its sandbox. The lead commits, pushes and opens the PR, naming Codex as
  builder in the body. It was cut off once by "model at capacity" and resumed cleanly.
- Two small builds cost about four times a Sonnet pass's tokens for the same size of
  change. Two rows is not a verdict; the scorecard will say.
- The seat earns its place on docs PRs too: on a stale-premise audit it found a test the
  auditor's own grep of the same file had missed.
- The model choice was an owner ruling: the Opus-equivalent tier by default, the top
  tier reserved for owner-invoked escalations such as the process audit.

Asks: `harness-init` generates a thin `AGENTS.md` and a `.codex/config.toml` when a
second vendor is configured; a `rules/second-vendor.md` or a section carrying the facts
above; the builder-seat commit workaround stated once.

## 6. Investigations are not build items; a project needs an observability instrument

The post-mortem of 2026-09-11 (`docs/review/harness-postmortem-2026-09-11.md` in Egypt)
is the largest single finding of the project. A one-line bug cost about 5M tokens, two
waves, nineteen role-sessions and about twenty rulings, because there was no cheap way
to see what the system did: every "why" question was a bespoke 100 to 300k probe, so
everyone reasoned from the code instead, and reasoning was wrong five times out of five.
A trace command (run one real input, print what every actor did per tick) turned each
question into a command; the owner's saved games loaded as fixtures found more defects
in an afternoon than the broad sweep found in a week. A later builder briefed on a
mission capstone, an investigation loop by nature, spent 0.86M because every run dumped
output into its context.

What generalises: the operating model now says "measure before mechanism" but the
plugin never asks what the project's instrument is, and never distinguishes the two
loops the post-mortem named: the investigation loop (one agent, the instrument, change a
line, rerun, no reviewer, no gate, until one paragraph says why) and the shipping loop
(the wave machinery, for a mechanism whose behaviour is understood).

Asks:
- `harness-init` asks, and `CLAUDE.md` records, how to replay a real input headlessly
  and what prints what happened. A project without one has that as its first issue.
- A rule: an investigation is never briefed to a builder as part of a build. It goes to
  a measuring role with a token ceiling, told to stop at the first measured red and
  report the binding link, and only then is a build briefed.
- A rule for run output: an agent that runs the system pipes it to a file and reads the
  summary, never the stream.

## 7. Test-suite shape: four kinds, derivations, and the stale-premise audit

Egypt's `.claude/rules/testing.md` and `team-judgment.md` are project-local but contain
nothing about games. They came from measured pain: 754 exact numeric pins across 61
files on a system with one shared random stream meant any change shifted unrelated pins,
each red pin needed a judgment, and three illegitimate re-pins were caught only because
a reviewer measured. A never-derived 20% win-rate floor blocked a fix for hours while its
denominator had grown by twenty adversarial rows and nobody asked whether it still meant
anything.

The rules that held:
- Four test kinds, each with what it may assert: rule tests (exact values fine when the
  value is the rule), invariants with the derivation in the doc comment (a floor with no
  derivation is a golden in disguise), goldens (exact seeded readings, change detectors,
  moved only with a stated cause per value), and fixtures (real inputs replayed, the
  per-PR gate). "Measured, therefore pinned" is refused in review.
- Every chance-based subsystem draws from its own seeded stream, so a change in one
  cannot shift pins in another.
- Before optimising for any number, trace it to a decision or a derivation; if you
  cannot, that is the finding: state it and stop.
- A stale-premise audit at milestone boundaries: read the rulings since the last one,
  find tests that still assert an overturned premise, file one finding per test. It works
  forward from the decisions log into the tests because comments carry no citations.
- Trigger 2: a reviewer's differential measurement showing a fix's premise false blocks
  another builder attempt until a model exists. It fired productively three times in
  one week (#561, #577, #587), each time within the hour, each time one held PR instead
  of a wrong ship. The 2026-09-11 decisions entry deferred proposing it upstream "if it
  proves out"; it has.

Ask: promote these to the plugin as a starter `rules/` set that `harness-init` copies
into `.claude/rules/`, with the stack-specific parts left as placeholders.

## 8. The comment linter is a mechanical rule that paid for itself

The rule "comments say why, never history" was prose-only for a week. When measured the
source tree was 44% comment lines, about a thousand citing an issue number, a date or a
ruling, and every builder paid to read them. `scripts/check-comments.mjs` hard-fails on
issue references, ISO and month-year dates and ruling or attribution phrases, reports
density per file, and runs in two modes: `--report` over the tree and `--diff` against
the default branch as a ratchet wired into lint. Cleanup cost about 2.5M tokens over
sixteen batches; the ratchet has held since. One false positive on the date pattern was
filed (#605). Separately, a lint rule now bans imports across the project's one
architectural boundary, which had been reviewer vigilance only.

Ask: ship the linter as a harness script with a configurable source root and comment
syntax, wired by `harness-init`, with the ratchet mode as the default and a note that a
comment can be load-bearing for a text-scanning test (one comment-only edit broke a
test that read the file as text).

## 9. Destructive-bash blocker: three recorded false positives, one fix

All fail closed, so they cost a denial and a rewrite, never a false allow. Recorded on
2026-09-11, 2026-09-12 and 2026-09-13:

- A `-f` anywhere in a command that also contains `git push` is read as a force push:
  `git push origin x && git worktree remove -f wt`, `rm -f`, `git worktree add -B`.
- A `+` in prose or arithmetic after the push token (`"+ 1"`) is read as a force
  refspec.
- A `HEAD:branch` refspec tripped it once.

The rm guard is already per-segment; the push guard is not. Ask: apply the same
segment split to the push checks so only tokens in the segment whose command word is
`git` and whose subcommand is `push` are inspected.

## 10. Stop gate and validate

- The Stop gate diffs the whole branch against the default branch, so a long-lived
  branch pays the full suite on every turn after the first code change, docs-only turns
  included. A "since the last green run on this head" check would remove most of the
  repeated runs. (The Codex audit's finding; not yet actioned.)
- `validate` lints once now. Good.
- The test-weakening guard is a tripwire for deletions and skips, not weakened
  assertions, and any matching ruling phrase passes it. The 2026-09-16 rules say so;
  the script's own header should too.

## 11. Worktree and merge hygiene, stated once

Each of these cost a session at least one incident. None is in the plugin yet.

- Never symlink dependencies into an agent worktree: `git add -A` commits the link, and
  a `.gitignore` entry ending in `/` matches only a directory.
- Push docs commits before any launch, or the builder's branch carries them and
  conflicts on merge.
- Keep a builder's or reviewer's worktree until its PR merges; a removed worktree is a
  lost agent (a reviewer could not be resumed for a rebase; an integrator could not be
  resumed at all). Stale worktrees then accumulate: eight from earlier sessions were
  still present at the last checkpoint. Ask: the checkpoint lists worktrees with no open
  PR and offers pruning.
- A merge script gates every cleanup step on the merged state: one branch was deleted
  under an open PR and restored from a local commit. Merging a stacked PR's base with the
  branch deleted closes the stacked PR.
- `gh pr merge` from a worktree fails when the main checkout also sits on the default
  branch; `-R owner/repo` avoids the local git operation.
- A reviewer given two sequential PRs waits on the lead's merge between them (95 minutes
  on one PR). Merge on sign-off, or give it a merge-independent second task.
- A builder that stalls at "no progress for 600s" with an intact uncommitted diff in its
  worktree is a stream death, not a hang: resume by agent id with "continue from your
  uncommitted state". Worked cleanly twice; no respawn.

## 12. Checkpoint: two lines worth adding

Egypt's checkpoints carry two lines the skill's step 6 does not ask for, and the owner's
stated priority ("getting me playing ASAP each session") depends on the first:

- **What the owner can use now**, with the command to run it and what changed since they
  last looked.
- **Asked of the owner, unanswered**, numbered, so the next session opens on them.

## 13. What the 2026-09-16 rewrite got right, on its first night

Confirmations, so they are not re-litigated:

- Capture and defer held: three owner observations were parked, discussed, none launched.
  The rule was written the day after the lead launched on observations it should have
  discussed.
- A composition experiment that stated its measured domain, and called the unmeasured
  case a prediction, cost one held PR when the prediction was refuted, not a wrong ship.
- A build against a model with reproduced probes needed no rework (#586); a build
  against an issue that looked like a flag needed its gate refuted once (#582).
- The owner's real inputs as fixtures are the per-PR gate. The broad sweep, demoted to
  wave end, was found to be unable to reach the mechanisms under change at all.
- Verbatim owner rulings with the lead's interpretation labelled: the relay is where the
  owner caught a corrupted ruling ("a floor of 1 unit" that was the lead's, not theirs).
- Rules only, under 130 lines, history in the decisions log. The owner's words: "poor
  instructions being read into context every single time. Do whatever we need to do."
  The `CLAUDE.md.template` still describes a two-layer file under 200 lines with inline
  prose; the rules-only shape is what held.
